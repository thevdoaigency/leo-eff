02 / The Split

Match Shots

Split-screens will do 80% of the persuasion in the final cut. That makes match shots the highest-value pixels of the day. Nail these first — before interviews, before beauty B-roll, before anything else.

How to Match

The Technique

  1. Load the "before" reference images on a phone or tablet. Bring both to every site.
  2. At the shot, hold the reference next to the viewfinder. Match camera position, height, and angle by eye.
  3. Shoot slightly wider than the reference — gives us room to reframe in edit.
  4. Locked-off static first. That's the split-screen master.
  5. If time, a second take with a slow push — for the beauty cut.
  6. Match the time of day if you can. Don't shoot golden hour if the reference is high noon.

Storyboard below is illustrative. The actual references will drive framing on the day.

Reference — On the Public Record

The Kickoff Tape

These are the specific lines and facts from the kickoff press conference that Gio and Justin's answers should pay off. When Gio or Justin lands a callback to one of these, that's what cuts together in the edit — and it works whether or not the kickoff video shows up in time.

Mayor Bibb
Announced a $21M investment across all five district stations. Said the city "won't spare any expense" on public safety facilities.
Chief Todd
4th district groundbreaking was May 1973. "Wear has taken a toll." Frames it as officer wellness, not just infrastructure.
Councilman
Called the conditions "deplorable" and "an embarrassment." Said for 25 years "the can has been kicked down the street." Then, publicly: "Now we'll move on to the firehouses."
Jimmy O'Malley — FOP
30 years on the force, "never walked into a building that is properly acclimated, doesn't have a leak." Officers spend "half their day" in the station. "The phone calls are going to stop."
Andy G — CPPA
Phone calls late at night "about the heating in the summertime and the cooling in the wintertime."
Eric Betts — Leopardo
Procured under Ohio Revised Code 717.02: Leopardo guarantees the savings or writes the city a check for the difference. "We've never missed a guarantee, and we're delivering, on average, 120% of our forecasted savings projections."

Five themes below, each with a target line. Not a script — a destination. If Gio or Justin lands a version of each one in their own words, we have the video.

01

Scale

$21M across five stations is the number the city already agreed to. That ratio to Rob's fire station ask does the persuasion work on its own — nobody has to make an argument if the numbers are said out loud.

"$21 million bought Cleveland five modernized police districts. Twenty fire stations is the same math on a bigger scale — and the city already knows we can run the machine."
02

Delivery

The union and council already told the ugly story. Gio and Justin's job is the payoff — what actually ended. Specific, concrete, personal.

"The 4th district hadn't been touched since 1973. Officers were working in a building older than most of them. Not anymore."
"The late-night phone calls the union president talked about at kickoff — heating in the summer, cooling in the winter — those aren't happening anymore."
03

Value

The 717.02 guarantee is the strongest risk-reversal in the whole pitch — Eric put it on the record at kickoff, but it needs to be said again, plainly, by the people who'd run the fire station work. Combined with the equivalency line, this is Value.

"Under 717.02 we guarantee the savings — or we write the city a check for the difference. On the police stations, we're delivering 120% of what we forecasted."
"That's the equivalent of powering every Cleveland fire station for a year." (once engineering delivers the equivalency)
04

Trust

The mayor's own words are the biggest gift here. Play them back. Officer wellness / recruiting is the underrated angle — the chief already opened it at kickoff, and the city is actively trying to hire.

"The mayor said Cleveland wouldn't spare any expense on public safety. This is what that bought."
"You can't recruit officers into buildings that are falling apart. Now you can."
05

Cleveland

The most important shift in this whole brief. The fire station work was already publicly promised at the police station kickoff, in front of the mayor. Rob is not making a cold ask — the city has already said the words on camera. Justin or Gio calling that back closes the loop.

"When we finished the police stations, the councilman said publicly: 'Now we'll move on to the firehouses.' We're ready when the city is."
04 / The Route

The Five Stations

Priority isn't equal. Sequence the day around transformation drama — which station changed the most is the flagship. Spend the most time there. It's the opener and closer of the final video.

  1. Flagship station (the one that changed the most): 90+ minutes. All match shots. Primary interview backdrop.
  2. Two secondary stations: 30-45 minutes each. Match shots only. Interviews if scheduling flexes.
  3. Remaining stations: 15-20 minutes each. Exterior match shot only. Skip if the day tightens.

Gio and Justin will know which is which. Trust their read on where the transformation is most photogenic.
