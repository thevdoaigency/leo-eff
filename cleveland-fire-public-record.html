Cleveland Fire & EMS — What's on the Public Record

We went looking for the finished modernization study. It isn't published. What turned up instead is a stronger paper trail: the fire work is already a named City program, and Cleveland's own procurement language ties it back to the police districts. Two things need your call before any of it goes on camera.

The 2024 modernization study has no published deliverable — no award, no phase reports, no "Station of the Future" concepts.

Document Ledger

What we can get, what's gone, and what needs a records request.

NOT PUBLISHED

Modernization Plan — consultant deliverable

Nothing in City press releases, Board of Control resolutions, Legistar, or trade press. No named consultant was ever awarded on the public record. Either the RFP wasn't awarded as scoped, or the work was absorbed into internal capital planning and never released.

URL DEAD

The May 2024 RFP itself

The City's hosted PDF returns a 404. Scope, phases and deliverables are still recoverable from indexed text — that's the reconstruction already on file.

RECORDS REQUEST

2024 CFD Facility Condition Report

All 25 fire stations plus the Training Academy on Lakeside and Division headquarters on Superior. Grades roofs, HVAC, plumbing, electrical and overall infrastructure. Nearly every building rated fair or poor in nearly every category; only seven of the 27 facilities inspected rated good on roofs. Also documented that just nine firehouses have separate facilities for female firefighters. Never posted publicly.

ON RECORD

December 2025 press coverage

The most useful public artifact we have. Quotes the condition report directly: mold remediation requiring demolition of basement office space at Fire Station 1 — which is also Division headquarters — HVAC there that can only be switched on or off rather than set to a temperature, and significant HVAC needs at nearly a dozen stations plus the Training Academy.

ON RECORD

City legislation & Board of Control minutes

Both the police contract authorization and the Fire/EMS enabling ordinance are public, named and citable. Sequence laid out below.

The Paper Trail

Fire/EMS is not a hypothetical next phase. It's a named City program with dates on it.

  • May 2024City issues the Fire and EMS Facility Modernization Plan RFP through the Mayor's Office of Capital Projects. No public award follows.
  • 2024CFD Facility Condition Report completed. Not released publicly.
  • Jul 2025Condition report obtained and reported by local investigative TV. City confirms a modernization plan is in progress and names its CFO as the person finding the money.
  • Sep 29, 2025Council passes Ordinance No. 846-2025 — the authority later cited for Fire/EMS work.
  • Nov 2025City issues the RFQ titled Public Safety Energy Improvements: Fire & EMS. Submissions run to mid-January 2026.
  • Dec 2025Press frames the fire work as the same financing approach used for the $21M five police district project, and names Leopardo as the firm selected for that police work.
  • Mar 25, 2026Board of Control approves NV5 Consultants as Owner's Representative for Public Safety Energy Improvements for Fire/EMS, not to exceed $31,999.

The naming is the finding. Police and Fire/EMS carry the same program title in City procurement: Public Safety Energy Improvements. On the record, the fire work reads as phase two of something Cleveland already started — not a new ask, and not a pitch we have to construct. That's firmer footing for the continuation framing than the press conference line on its own.

Two Things to Settle

Both are blocking. Neither should reach a script until it's answered.

HoldWhat does "first reading" mean on Sept 30?

Read against the record, "first reading" plus "close mid-October" sounds less like reviewing a consultant's study and more like a legislative first reading. Cleveland's ESPC path runs through a Council ordinance that names the ESCO outright — which is exactly how the police contract was authorized.

The two readings imply completely different events, different footage, different timing. If it's legislative, Sept 30 is the moment the Leopardo name lands publicly on the fire deal. That's a shoot, not a calendar note.

Owner · Rob / confirm with Curtis

DecideLeopardo Energy or Leopardo Efficiency?

Every piece of Cleveland legislation and press coverage says Leopardo Energy, LLC. Campaign materials say Leopardo Efficiency. Anyone who pulls the ordinance to verify a claim will hit that gap.

Need a decision on which name carries in public-facing copy, and whether video credits and the site reconcile the two.
