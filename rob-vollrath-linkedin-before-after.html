About

Most cities have the same quiet problem: the buildings their police officers and firefighters depend on are aging out, and there's rarely budget to fix them without raising taxes or cutting somewhere else.

That's the problem I solve.

At Leopardo Efficiency, we help cities rebuild and modernize the facilities their first responders work from — and we fund it through the guaranteed energy and operational savings those upgrades produce. The savings aren't the product; they're the financing. The outcome is mission-critical infrastructure: stations that are safe, modern, and built to last, delivered without asking a city council to choose between a levy and a firehouse.

In Cleveland, that meant rebuilding five police district stations for the City of Cleveland and its Division of Police — modern facilities delivered on the strength of guaranteed savings, with zero missed guarantees across our portfolio. When a project promises a city a number, we stand behind it.

The same approach has modernized fire stations for other cities — the facility changes, the guarantee doesn't.

That track record is why the conversation usually starts the same way: a city has a police station or a firehouse that can't wait much longer, and they need a partner who can make the math work. My job is to make sure the answer is yes — and that the guarantee behind it holds.

Before Leopardo, I spent a decade leading green building solutions and business development in performance contracting, so I've sat on every side of these deals: development, engineering, financing, and the measurement that proves the savings are real.

Something unique: outside of work, I write screenplays. I co-wrote and starred in a short film that won multiple film festival awards in 2006. Structuring a complex deal and building a story have more in common than you'd think — both come down to knowing what the audience needs before they do.

If your city is staring down a first-responder facility that needs to happen, let's talk.
