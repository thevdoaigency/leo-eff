The pitch, as sold

Strip the language and the proposal is this: use "AI" to manufacture reach — ten thousand connections or followers aimed at the portfolio companies, fast, at scale. It sounds like 2026.

It isn't. Every version of it, once you take the costume off, is the same thing that's been getting accounts flagged for a decade. This is dressed-up bot farming.

01

The wall you can't buy past

~100/wk
LinkedIn's connection-invite cap. Per account. Identical on every paid tier — no tier you can buy raises it. That's roughly 5,200 real invitations a year, maximum, from one real person sending real requests.

So ten thousand connections cannot come from one real account behaving normally. The only paths to the number are to fake the accounts, fake the sending, or both. The cap is the tell: the moment the goal becomes a big number instead of the right names, you've left strategy and entered fraud mechanics.

02

The costume, in three sizes

COSTUME 01

Automation tools

Expandi · Dripify · Waalaxy · PhantomBuster
Auto-view, auto-follow, auto-invite, auto-DM — with an LLM writing the "loved your post" line. Still slams into the 100/week wall. Push past it and the account gets throttled.
Costume: "AI personalization." Reality: a spam bot with better grammar.
COSTUME 02

Account fleets

Rotated seats · synthetic identities
Run a rack of accounts — real or fabricated — to multiply the cap. The "AI" manages cadence across the fleet so no single account trips the wire.
Costume: "scaled outreach." Reality: a drawer of sock puppets.
COSTUME 03

Bought followers

Click farms · diffusion-model faces
Buy bulk followers for a company page: dormant or synthetic profiles with AI-generated headshots and AI-written bios. Instant, cheap, and 100% fake.
Costume: "audience growth." Reality: 10,000 avatars who sign nothing.
03

Why ten thousand is worth zero

01

No decision authority

The buyer is a named human on a municipal committee — city manager, mayor, facilities or energy director, council member, ESCO consultant. A bot cannot influence or sign a public contract. In a real market of a few thousand deciders, 10,000 is mostly non-buyers and non-humans by construction.

02

No mechanism to revenue

An 8-figure ESPC is a 12–24 month sale, gated by procurement, MBE/FBE/CSB certification, references, and a council vote. There is no step in that chain where a fake follower becomes a dollar.

03

The asset evaporates

LinkedIn sweeps fake accounts on its own schedule. The 10,000 depreciates toward zero and drags real account health — the president's actual profile, with the real connections — down with it.

04

It reads as fraud to the only people who matter

We sell trust for public infrastructure. A city manager — or a competitor running bid diligence — sees bot avatars trailing the page and reads one word: fake. One screenshot in a rival's hands during procurement is a self-inflicted kill shot.

05

It burns the only scarce resource

Even a real sliver would waste the finite business-development hours that belong to the named buyers. Attention is the constraint on this sale. This lights it on fire chasing a vanity count.

06

It fails the CFO's own test

Cost with zero attributable pipeline. Apply the same ROI bar we'd demand of any real outreach motion and this dies on contact — there is nothing to attribute.

The verdict

Real strategy has a mechanism. Vanity growth has a number.

A single Cleveland relationship draws a clean line to an 8-figure incremental deal — champion → trigger event → warm intro → procurement → council vote → signature. The 10K-avatar model cannot name one step from a bot to a dollar. That isn't a close call. It's the difference between a pipeline and a costume.

The honest version of "reach": saturate the real named list — targeted ads and thought-leader posts off the presidents' own accounts, pointed at the ~thousand people who can actually sign. Reach aimed at the list — not a list manufactured to fake reach.
