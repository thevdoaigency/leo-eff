How to read this & what's cleared

Timecodes
The in-point to find each take in the transcript/footage. Where a speaker did multiple passes, the cleanest complete take is the one selected.
[ Brackets ]
Fix speech-to-text garble of proper nouns — the transcripts mangle the company name (rendered "Leah Pardo," "Picardo," etc.). Spoken words are otherwise untouched.
… ellipsis
Trimmed false starts and filler ("probably shouldn't say that," repeated words). Nothing added.
Curtis lines
From the approved CEO script (C-numbers), assumed in the can. No timecode. Trim to the marked in-point where noted.
Figures — use
$21M across all five districts and the guarantee mechanism (Ohio Revised Code 717.02 — savings cover the cost or Leopardo writes the check). Public record.
Figures — held
$108,000/yr and "north of $7M/yr" (Justin) stay out of every cut — he flagged the first on tape. None of these four need them.
Guardrail
Harsh condition language ("deplorable," leaks, "1970s") rides on city and union voices — never Leopardo's own people.
01

Built by Cleveland, for Cleveland

union labor · ~75–90s · local + labor + LinkedIn

The work that fixes these stations is done by Cleveland's own union trades — the project reinvests in the community it serves.

—Cold open · B-roll
Union crews on site — trades working, tools, hands. No VO. Let sound breathe, then bring up the first bite.
01TC 00:03:36Giovanni Luciano · PM
"Massive undertaking doing the Cleveland Police District renovations. We could not have done it without the signatory union contractors from the Cleveland Building Trades Council. They were an outstanding help, and we could not have done it without them."
Cut against: wide of a district exterior, then crews inside.
02TC 00:01:22Justin Jezierski · PM
"So we had a lot of local trades involved in this project, from sheet metal to carpenters to painters to flooring guys to laborers in general. … It was really important to us to make sure that we were using … good quality union work to get this job accomplished in a timely manner and under budget."
Cut against: each trade doing its work — sheet metal, paint, flooring.
03TC 00:02:00Justin Jezierski · PM
"We couldn't have done this project without the help of the unions and their great stewardship … providing us a great contracting network to work within."
04TC 00:02:00Justin Jezierski · PM
"… part of the guarantees that we make … is we create local jobs that stay local to the specific areas by hiring the unions and hiring good quality union workers for the city of Cleveland."
Key line. This is the economic argument — local money staying local.
05TC 00:02:27Justin Jezierski · PM
"… we were able to make that commitment to the unions in the city that we would utilize good quality local union contractors and have the work done by Cleveland residents."
06Curtis Leopardo · C2 (tail)
"… we start with first responders, because a police station or a firehouse is something a community can't do without. The savings is the mechanism; a better city — starting with the people who protect it — is the mission."
In-point: start at "we start with first responders…" — lifts the piece from a jobs story to a civic-value story.
07TC 00:02:07Drake Wahlers · SVP Engineering
"We're not just another construction company coming in to repair your buildings. We are subject matter experts."
08TC 00:04:17Giovanni Luciano · PM
"This is a huge step forward and a huge show of good faith that they are here to support everybody who comes into these public safety buildings. … The city is looking out for our public safety officials, and we are more than happy to be the driving arm of that."
09TC 00:06:00Giovanni Luciano · PM
"It's a great opportunity for me to give back to the brave men and women that are out there serving our city. I'm a proud resident of Cleveland."
Cut against: finished interior, then pull to the union crew that built it. End card.
02

The Seat I Sat In

buyer-turned-believer · ~2 min · finance directors + councils

The person who de-risks this for a nervous finance director sat in that exact seat — and was skeptical too.

—Cold open
Heather to camera, or walking a station corridor. Open cold on the skepticism.
01TC 00:01:02Heather Garay · Partnership Executive
"… many of the people I talk to have the same response I had the first time … that simply that this project was just too good to be true."
02TC 00:00:08Heather Garay
"I met [Leopardo] when I was working as the finance director for a community in … Indiana. They came to our community and approached us with an innovative way to upgrade facilities, fleet and our entire infrastructure with cost saving[s]."
Establishes the arc: she was the customer first.
03TC 00:01:17Heather Garay
"I remember a time when Drake, [now] our senior VP of engineering, came to my office, and my … staff at the time encouraged me to just give him our utility bills … to organize. They were a headache for the city … [He] came back with organized spreadsheets and contacted the vendor and got us all of our accounts lined up perfectly. And that's when I really started to think these guys could help us."
Cut against: spreadsheets, utility bills, a desk. The concrete proof moment.
04TC 00:01:41Heather Garay
"I began to really believe in the solution, and the city went on to do three successful phases."
05TC 00:02:06Heather Garay
"… when I asked to join the team at [Leopardo] … we worked together to create a spot for me where I could use my legal background and my municipal finance background to really help other communities go through this process more smoothly."
The turn: customer → believer → joined.
06TC 00:03:38Heather Garay
"… with the city of Cleveland … it was not only that this was the first time that they were going to issue debt of this nature, but really, they put their faith in us to walk them through this process and upgrade their … stations."
07Curtis Leopardo · C4
"… because the work is done under an Energy Savings Performance Contract, that savings is guaranteed by state code — if it ever falls short, we cover the difference, not the city. So the money question, which is where most of these projects die, is handled."
In-point: start at "because the work is done under…" — the de-risk answering her "faith" beat, in the CEO's voice.
08Curtis Leopardo · C8
"When a city trusts the Leopardo name with a police station or a firehouse, they're trusting us with something a whole community organizes its sense of safety around. … The name means we're still standing behind that building long after the work is done."
09TC 00:02:28Heather Garay
"I work with the customer community from the very first meeting, all the way through implementation and follow up … We're there to help navigate that process for them and really … be a support system throughout."
Close on Heather with council/finance staff on a site walk. End card.
03

Second Home

human / morale · ~90s · social + recruiting

These are the buildings where first responders spend half their lives — making them worthy changes morale and recruiting.

—Cold open · before-state B-roll
Before footage — dated interiors, leaks, worn workstations — under the first union voice.
01Presser · TC 00:06:32Jimmy O'Malley · FOP President
"I've been a Cleveland policeman for almost 30 years, and I've never walked into a building that is properly acclimated, doesn't have a leak. … They're working 12 hour shifts. Half their day is spent in a police building."
Guardrail-safe: condition language on a union voice, not Leopardo's.
02Presser · TC 00:05:34Andy · Cleveland Police Patrol Assoc.
"There's a lot of times me and Jimmy O'Malley … get phone calls late at night about the heating in the summertime and the cooling in the wintertime."
03TC 00:04:45Heather Garay
"… when you come back to a facility where the walls are leaking, the roof … needs repairs … no updated lighting — these are signals that no one cares. And when you make these improvements, it really shows that the community is invested in improving the work environment for these individuals who play such a critical role."
—Turn · before → after
Match-cut the same space renovated. The visual pivot lands here.
04TC 00:02:47Justin Jezierski · PM
"When an officer walks into the station after it's been renovated, I want them to feel like it's a second home to them. I want them to be cool in the summer, and I want them to be warm in the winter. And I also want them to have a nice looking facility …"
Title line. "Second home."
05TC 00:02:15Giovanni Luciano · PM
"What we want to see from these renovations is a wow factor. Everybody walks in and sees that this is something that the city is putting effort into. The city cares about the employees."
06Curtis Leopardo · C3 (tail)
"… if that building is decades behind, we've quietly told the people who protect us that they come last. We don't believe they should."
In-point: start at "if that building is decades behind…" — the moral center in the CEO's voice.
07TC 00:04:09Heather Garay
"… they were having challenges attracting new recruits and attaining the quality recruits … the stations were a big part of that. So by upgrading these facilities, the community was able to show that they were investing in those police officers and are now attracting more qualified individuals. … To me, that's an unmarked savings, but something that's a really big value."
Her signature. Recruiting/retention — the value nobody else names.
08TC 00:04:45Giovanni Luciano · PM
"Now officers, they're coming into work and they have a brand new interior, a very nice place to work. And I think that really lifts the morale of the officers …"
Close on officers in the renovated space. Optional tag → House Jones, "public servants … treated with the dignity and respect" (Presser TC 00:11:00).
04

Twenty Years From Now

built to last · ~75–90s · skeptics + procurement

Not a patch job — infrastructure built to outlast the people who installed it, backed by a name that stands behind it.

—Cold open · B-roll
New systems going in — HVAC units, solar arrays on the roof, new plumbing. The hardware.
01TC 00:03:52Giovanni Luciano · PM
"We've got a ton of new HVAC upgrades here and solar arrays that have been installed, lots of new plumbing infrastructure upgrades. … The equipment that we installed today is going to last us 20 years. Easy — given the right amount of preventative maintenance and upkeep."
Say "heating and cooling" in captions if "HVAC" reads as an abbreviation on screen.
02TC 00:01:29Giovanni Luciano · PM
"… bringing in all this new solar panels for the roof, all new solar arrays for each of the different buildings … a new roof system for district five. … Many people were very much looking forward to not having a leaky roof."
03TC 00:03:16Justin Jezierski · PM
"… 20 years from now, we feel that all of the equipment will be still in the same condition … We want to make sure that 20 years from now the air conditioning is still cooling, the heating still heating, and the walls and all the doors and all the finishes still look very similar to where we left."
04TC 00:04:03Justin Jezierski · PM
"… the equipment that we've installed will last over 20 to 30 years, and … provide them with efficient heating, cooling, lighting conditions … and a facility that they can be proud of and that they can call home."
05TC 00:02:35Drake Wahlers · SVP Engineering
"We're not only helping you fix your buildings, we're setting your buildings up for the future … [using] guaranteed savings to help pay for upgrades to set cities up for the future."
06Curtis Leopardo · C8 (tail)
"The name means we're still standing behind that building long after the work is done — because my family's name is standing behind it too."
In-point: the stewardship line lands the durability argument on the CEO.
07Curtis Leopardo · C9 (tail)
"If we get that right, a lot of first responders end up in buildings worthy of what we ask of them, and a lot of cities fund things they thought they couldn't."
Close on the finished systems running. End card.

It's Not a Cleveland Story · spinoff

national · ~45–60s · other cities / mayors

Cleveland is the proof of a repeatable pattern — pointed at every other city that has the same buildings and the same problem.

01Curtis Leopardo · C5
"Cleveland is five police stations, delivered — a twenty-one-million-dollar investment across all five districts — but it would be a mistake to hear that as a Cleveland story. … Cleveland just proved the pattern works at scale."
02TC 00:02:07Drake Wahlers · SVP Engineering
"… cities like Speedway, Indiana, South Euclid, Ohio and Cleveland, Ohio speak to our credibility in delivery and delivery of guaranteed savings."
03Curtis Leopardo · C6
"… no one on a city council wants to be the person who tells first responders their building doesn't matter. … That's not a sales angle — it's just true, and it's true in every city."
04Curtis Leopardo · C10
"When a city's police or fire facilities need to be updated or built new, Leopardo should be the first call … We've proven it in Cleveland and beyond."
This close matches the R10/H10 line — intercuttable once Rob and Heather are on tape.
