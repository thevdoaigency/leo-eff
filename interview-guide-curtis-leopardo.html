The one job

What the CEO sit-down must land

Curtis is the only voice who can say why the company exists to do this at all. Rob owns the authority and the guarantee; Heather owns the buyer's trust; the PMs own the build. Curtis owns the mission above all three: this is first-responder infrastructure, and energy savings is only how a city pays for it. His job is to lift Cleveland from a project into a national pattern — nearly every city has these buildings, and none can tell its first responders they don't deserve working ones — and to put the Leopardo name behind that promise.

02

Production standard

read before rolling
1
Restate the question in the answer. Answers must stand alone. "The reason we build around police and fire facilities…" — not "Because…". We only keep his voice, not ours.
2
No abbreviations on camera. Say "Energy Savings Performance Contract," "first responders," "heating and cooling" — not the acronyms. (Same note that tripped up the PM takes.)
3
Say the full company name once, cleanly. Confirm the exact legal name at the top of the sit-down — speech-to-text has been mangling it badly, so a clean on-camera read helps captions too.
4
One idea per answer. The vision questions invite long answers — if he stacks three thoughts, ask for the best one again on its own. Short, self-contained takes cut into everything downstream.
5
Guardrail the language. "Update or new construction," never "failing" or "deplorable" in Leopardo's own voice — the city can say that about its old buildings; we describe the fix, not the failure.
6
Figures: use only what's cleared. See the figures table below. The CEO answers lean company-wide — those claims especially can't go on camera until they're green.

C1
Tell me who you are and what you do at Leopardo Efficiency.
For: clean intro card — name, title, one line on leading the company.
The mission — why this work
C2
When you describe what Leopardo does, you don't call it an energy company. How do you describe it?
For: the reframe — this is public-safety / first-responder infrastructure; energy savings is the financing, not the product. THE thesis line.
C3
Why build a company around police stations and firehouses specifically?
For: the conviction — first responders as mission-critical; the human stakes behind the buildings.
C4
You've said the savings is the mechanism, not the mission. Unpack that for someone hearing it for the first time.
For: savings-funds-the-facility at CEO altitude — outcome over mechanism. Pairs with Rob R2 but stays at vision level; don't re-explain the code.
The national pattern — why this scales
C5
Cleveland is five police stations, delivered. Why isn't this a Cleveland story?
For: nearly every city in America has these facilities and the same problem; Cleveland is proof of a repeatable pattern.
C6
What is it about a police or fire facility that makes this an easier "yes" for a city than almost any other public project?
For: the strategic wedge in his words — no council wants to deny first responders working buildings. The insight that makes it national.
C7
A mayor or city manager watching this — what's the pattern you want them to recognize in their own city?
For: the "call us first" positioning at CEO altitude — the invitation, not the pitch.
The name & the mandate
C8
Your name is on this. What does it mean that a city trusts the Leopardo name with something this essential?
For: stewardship, legacy, the weight of the name — unique to the CEO. No one else on the slate can say this.
C9
What is Leopardo Efficiency actually building toward — five years out, ten?
For: the ambition and the national mission; where this goes beyond any one contract.
The authority close
C10
When a city's police or fire facilities need to be updated or built new, why should Leopardo be the first call?
For: THE positioning line — same as R10 and H10, now in the CEO's voice. Watch the guardrail — "update or new construction," not "failing."
